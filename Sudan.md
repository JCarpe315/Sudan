### THE SETUP — WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW BEFORE THE FIRST PAGE OF EVIDENCE

**1. Two armies, one country.** The Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) is the national army — the institution of the state. The Rapid Support Forces (RSF) is something else: it began as the **Janjaweed** — "devils on horseback," the mounted Arab militias the Bashir government armed in 2003–2004 to crush the Darfur rebellion, and the target of the International Criminal Court's first Sudan warrants. In 2013, President Omar al-Bashir folded those militias into a single paramilitary — a private army loyal to a person rather than a state — under **Mohamed Hamdan "Hemedti" Dagalo**, a camel-trader-turned-militia-chief from the Rizeigat tribe. The design had a purpose: such a force fights your wars in Yemen, guards your capital, and owes everything to you. In 2019 Hemedti turned on Bashir and helped overthrow him. In 2021 he and the army chief, **Abdel Fattah al-Burhan**, jointly overthrew the civilian government they had promised to hand power to. On **April 15, 2023**, the two generals went to war with each other over a single question: would the RSF merge into the army — Burhan said within two years; Hemedti demanded ten? Every mechanism documented in this report descends from that question. A militia that refuses to disband must pay itself, arm itself, and replace its losses — and it does all three, in documented ways, with children.

**2. What "disappeared" means.** Three different realities share this one word, and keeping them separate is the whole discipline of this document. A child is **unrecorded-dead** when their body is buried without a name or a grave that can be found. A child is **taken** when they are alive in someone else's hands — as a soldier, a herder, a ransom, or a bride. A child is **unregistered** when no document anywhere says they exist. One child can be all three in sequence: taken, then erased from the record, then dead. Most of the world's statistics count only one category at a time, which is one reason the true scale is unknowable by design.

**3. Who's who.** The **Masalit** are a non-Arab farming people of West Darfur, targeted at El Geneina. The **Zaghawa** are a non-Arab people straddling the Chad border, targeted at Zamzam and El Fasher. The **Fur** are Darfur's largest non-Arab people. The **Rizeigat** and **Misseriya** are Arab cattle-herding peoples — together, the **Baggara** — whose young men fill RSF ranks and whose herding economy creates the standing demand for captive labor that this document traces across two centuries. The **Nuba** are non-Arab peoples of the South Kordofan mountains. Knowing these names matters, because the pattern of who is taken and who takes is not random — it is the map itself.

**4. Tasis.** *Tasis* (Arabic: "foundation") is the RSF's parallel government — the self-declared "Government of Peace and Unity," chartered in Nairobi in February 2025 and announced in Nyala that July. It has a prime minister, a cabinet, governors, exams, and its own currency. No country recognizes it. It matters because it is building the administrative machinery — including child-welfare and identity-issuing functions — that would absorb a generation of undocumented children if it endures. (Full detail: Part V.2.)

**5. The recurring terms, in plain language.**
- **UASC** — unaccompanied or separated child: a child without a parent or caregiver, or separated from them in flight.
- **Grave violations** — the UN's six defined war-crimes-against-children categories: killing and maiming; recruitment as soldiers; rape and sexual violence; abduction; attacks on schools and hospitals; denial of humanitarian access.
- **CAAC** — the UN's Children and Armed Conflict mechanism, which maintains the annual "list of shame."
- **Faza'a** — a pre-Islamic Sudanese tribal tradition of rallying kin for war or revenge; in this war, repurposed as a recruitment engine.
- **Kafala** — the Islamic-law care arrangement under which a family assumes legal responsibility for a child without adopting them; it preserves the child's lineage (Nasab) and, with it, a permanent second-class status. In Egypt it is the legal door through which informal absorption of Sudanese children passes.
- **CRSV** — conflict-related sexual violence.
- **OTP / ASP** — the ICC's Office of the Prosecutor; the Assembly of States Parties, the court's governing parliament of member countries.
- **FFM** — the UN Fact-Finding Mission on Sudan; **ACHPR** — the African Commission's own joint fact-finding mission. (Full machinery: Part VIII.)

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Between April 2023 and September 2026, Sudan's civil war produced the largest displacement crisis in the world — over 13.6 million people uprooted, more than 4 million of them fleeing across borders — and within that crisis, a quieter, deliberate event: **the systematic disappearance of children**. This document assembles the full public record of that event: how it happens, who does it, who pays for it, who documents it, who has failed to stop it, and where the system that permits it can be broken.

**The core findings:**

1. **Children disappear through four documented mechanisms** that operate simultaneously and reinforce one another: mass killing and forced family separation (El Fasher, October 2025 — assessed by the UN Fact-Finding Mission as bearing "the hallmarks of genocide"); abduction into RSF custody for slavery, child soldiering, ransom, and sexual servitude (a 26-witness Reuters investigation documented at least 56 children, ages two months to seventeen years); trafficking outflow through Egyptian and Libyan corridors toward exploitation markets; and institutional absorption — orphanage children, unaccompanied minors, and the unregistered placed into informal foster care with no paper trail (the Mygoma case, where approximately 275 children were dispersed into foster homes across three states with no public registry).

2. **The method is two hundred years old, industrialized.** The abduction of children for herding labor, sexual slavery, and sale is the reactivation of the Baggara slave-raiding complex documented continuously from the Turkiyya era through 1983–2005 Darfur to the present war. What has changed is scale, profit, and deniability: children are now recruited for a documented **$232–475 bounty per child** paid to "mayors" and tribal brokers; an estimated 8,000–10,000 child soldiers serve the RSF; ransom economies around El Fasher mirror the methods of Libyan trafficking gangs down to the video-call beatings.

3. **The war finances itself through gold, and the gold is laundered into legitimacy.** Over 80% of Sudan's gold — 74.6 tonnes produced in 2025, of which roughly 12.5 tonnes reached the public treasury — is smuggled, with 96–97% flowing to the United Arab Emirates. A documented corporate network converts it: front companies in Dubai, four Swiss refineries exploiting the "last country of processing" loophole (316 tonnes, CHF 27 billion, January–September 2025), and end-buyers including major Western consumer brands. The UAE corporate and state apparatus documented in this record — named individuals, companies, banks, aviation assets, and a court that prosecutes only one side's smuggling — constitutes the single most important external enabler of the war.

4. **Every accountability institution has failed exactly where Sudan needs it, in ways that are documented and named.** The ICC shelved a Darfur warrant application while its prosecutor was removed by member states; the UN Panel of Experts' final report was procedurally blocked from consideration by a UAE-aligned council member; the International Court of Justice dismissed Sudan's case against the UAE on a jurisdictional reservation; the UN Security Council's sanctions regime expires in its current form on October 9, 2026; Sudan's own courts have sentenced the RSF leadership to death in absentia with no custody mechanism; and the International Committee of the Red Cross holds 11,000+ missing-persons cases that include no published child-specific accounting.

5. **The system has identifiable weak points, and several are already cracking.** US Treasury has named the Colombian recruitment chain, a Panama recruiting company's officers, and an Indian explosives manufacturer; the EU and UK have banned Sudanese gold and sanctioned the laundering network; Switzerland has aligned; Kenya's gold-export gap to the UAE has been quantified at +1,719%; the UAE's air bridge was bombed shut on its northern route by Egypt in early 2026; a defector cascade is stripping the RSF of commanders and delivering evidence; a Kenyan universal-jurisdiction complaint — the first in that country's history — names ten RSF members; and the ICC's acting leadership announced in July 2026 "linkage evidence" connecting crimes to "specific persons in leadership mode." Eighteen days from this document's compilation, the Security Council vote of October 9, 2026, will decide whether kidnapping-for-ransom becomes a sanctionable offense in its own right.

**Who this document is for:** investigators, journalists, litigators, policymakers, and researchers who need the whole picture in one place — the names, the money, the mechanisms, the failures, and the pressure points — with every claim traceable to its source.

## PART I — THE CHRONOLOGY: HOW THE DISAPPEARANCE ECOLOGY WAS BUILT

*What happened, in what order, and where the children were when it happened. Every mechanism named later in this document — the bounties, the gold, the detention archipelago, the laundering — first appears here as an event with a date. Read this part for the shape of the thing: how a war between two generals became, within thirty months, a system for making children disappear.*

### Phase I — April–June 2023: Khartoum Falls, the Institutions Are Exposed

**April 15, 2023.** War begins between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) under Gen. Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) under Gen. Mohamed Hamdan "Hemedti" Dagalo — a war between the two generals who had jointly overthrown the civilian government in October 2021. Khartoum's civilian institutions are caught in the first crossfire [S/A].

**April–May 2023 — Mygoma Orphanage.** Sudan's largest orphanage, the al-Mygoma Home for Orphaned Children (established 1961, receiving hundreds of babies annually, mostly from unwed mothers under conditions of extreme stigma), is trapped with 370 children inside as fighting engulfs Khartoum. Over 50 babies die — 13–14 in a single 24-hour period on May 26 — from dehydration, heat, and hunger; some die in their cribs while roughly 20 nannies remain [A: WSJ; UNICEF]. The death-toll record conflicts: Reuters reported ≥50 by May 29; the volunteer group Hadhreen confirmed 71; UNICEF's figure was 67; Khartoum state minister Siddiq Freni later said the toll reached 69 and that "at some points, the state was losing around 14 children a day" [A/B: Asharq Al-Awsat, Aug 10, 2026]. No official toll was ever reconciled — **anomaly A1** (see Part XIII).

**June 7, 2023 — First evacuation.** ICRC and UNICEF evacuate the orphanage: 280 children plus 70 caretakers in the ICRC count, 297 in UNICEF's, roughly 300 by the next morning, "many of the children under age two." Critically, "some of the new arrivals didn't have birth certificates or names… the work to get them properly documented continued for weeks." The children are moved to a transit center in Wad Madani and placed under Ministry of Social Development custody pending foster placement — meaning hundreds of effectively anonymous infants entered Sudan's informal foster system in the middle of a collapsing state [S: UNICEF; A: Reuters, WSJ]. The 280/297/300 discrepancy is unexplained in any source — **anomaly A2**.

**June 2023 — El Geneina, West Darfur.** The RSF and allied Arab militias (the Tamazuj/"Third Front" and Rizeigat fighters) massacre Masalit civilians. Named perpetrators in the record: **Abdel Rahman Juma** (RSF West Darfur commander, US-sanctioned), **Idriss Hassan**, **Musa Angir** (Tamazuj commander), **Massar Abdelrahman Assil** (Rizeigat emir, EU-sanctioned), **Hafiz Hassan**, **Tijani Karshoum**. Up to 15,000 are killed per UN estimates; more than 370,000 flee to Chad. Human Rights Watch (May 2024) places command responsibility on Hemedti, his brother **Abdel Raheem Hamdan Dagalo**, and **Joma'a Barakallah** [S: HRW, UN]. On June 14, West Darfur governor **Khamis Abakar** is executed by Juma's men.

**June 25, 2023.** Lawyers file 450+ allegations of enforced disappearance with the Madani prosecutor; the **Missing Initiative** (co-founder Fadia Khalaf) has already documented 305 missing persons, including some 20 minors, using Facebook as a de facto registry [B: Missing Initiative].

### Phase II — 2023–2024: Recruitment, Occupation, and the Loot Economy

**Khartoum under RSF rule (22 months).** The RSF holds the capital until the SAF recapture of March 2025. The occupation record, assembled by OHCHR, photojournalists, and the Sudanese Archive, includes: **Soba Prison**, where 6,000+ civilians were held for 22 months, forced into looting crews, with children as young as 13 detained alongside adults and children as young as 14 used as guards by the RSF; churches converted to detention and torture sites; makeshift cemeteries that are still being exhumed; and an estimated **522,000 child deaths from malnutrition between April 2023 and January 2025** (Sudan Doctors Union figure) [S: OHCHR, March 6, 2025; A: Giles Clarke/PHA; B: Sudan Doctors Union]. On December 21, 2024, a Misseriya RSF faction stormed Soba to free detained fighters — a mass escape event whose fates are unrecorded [B: Sudan Tribune].

**Child recruitment from the first months.** The Africa Centre for Justice and Peace Studies (ACJPS), working inside Nyala in 2023, documents the recruitment layer by name: recruiters paid **250,000–400,000 SDG ($232–357) per child**; named recruiters include **Major Sadam Mohamed Yagoup Al-Nazir** (RSF Military Intelligence), **Mohamed Zain** (recruited ~200 at once, majority children, October 28, 2023), Sheikh "Ajeeb" (tribal leader, Kabra), and **Abdallah Idris "Bata"** (community leader and mayor, Kas locality). Recruitment catchments mapped: Tembisko, Bulbul Abu Jazo, Sania Deliba, Kabra, Adal al-Fursan, Umm Jannah, Umm Zaifa, Umm Dafouk, Umm Dukhan, Rhyd al-Bardi, Furbarnaqa (South Darfur); Khazan Jadid (Sheria locality); Natika and Kas (west of Nyala) [B: ACJPS, Dec 18, 2023]. The UN Secretary-General verified 16 RSF child-recruitment cases in 2024; the 2025 Children and Armed Conflict annexes list the RSF, SAF, and six other groups [S].

**The UAE–Black Shield precedent.** In January 2020 — three years before the war — roughly 300 Sudanese men were recruited by the Emirati company Black Shield as "security guards," given three months of military training, and sent to Haftar-held Libya; 175 returned after family protests [B: Dabanga]. This is the direct ancestor of the GSSG/Colombian pipeline documented in Part V.

### Phase III — April–October 2025: Zamzam and the Siege of El Fasher

**April 11, 2025 — Zamzam.** The RSF attacks Zamzam IDP camp, which held an estimated 700,000 displaced people. The verified record: **at least 1,013 killed** (OHCHR, December 18, 2025), including **319 summarily executed** in homes, the market, schools, health facilities, and mosques; 104 documented sexual-violence survivors (75 women, 26 girls, 3 boys, mostly Zaghawa); families fed children peanut shells; 26 suppliers executed on the Zamzam–Tawila road. A Guardian investigation (August 2025) found the local emergency committee confirmed **1,500+ deaths and hundreds more unaccounted for**; a Darfur atrocity expert was "certain" the toll exceeded 2,000 [S: OHCHR; A: The Guardian]. During the assault and the evacuation that followed, RSF-allied militia abducted **40 aid workers and roughly fifty evacuating civilians**. The camp was converted into an RSF military base; NASA FIRMS satellite data recorded 88 heat anomalies consistent with arson; the Conflict Intelligence Research team verified one RSF fighter appearing in three separate violation videos [A/B].

**The abduction that defines the case.** At the camp's southern entrance on April 11, **roughly fifty teenage girls were bundled into RSF pickups. The Guardian's reporting states: "None have been seen since."** Separately, eleven women were taken to the **Tabit** area on April 13 and ransomed for 15 million SDG (~$8,000); the Strategic Initiative for Women in the Horn of Africa (SIHA) documented 64 abduction and enforced-disappearance cases of women and girls from Zamzam (verification ongoing), with abductees reportedly moved to **Arab Bashir, Al-Awna, and Um Jalbakh** [A: The Guardian; B: SIHA]. In the weeks after, 138 children under fifteen were treated for gunshot and shrapnel wounds in Tawila [A]. As of this document's compilation, **no documented rescue or return of the Zamzam abductees has been located** — **gap G1**.

**Why Tabit matters.** Tabit is not a random location. On **October 31, 2014**, Sudanese Armed Forces garrison troops carried out a mass rape of more than 200 women and girls in Tabit; the UN-AU mission's compromised investigation produced no accountability [A: HRW/UN record]. In 2025–2026 the same locality functions as an RSF holding site for abducted women: the BBC's "Amira" account (August 7, 2026) describes being blindfolded, taken to Tabit, held two months, raped daily, left pregnant, and dumped near her capture site [A: BBC]. Tabit is a twelve-year, two-regime rape camp. That continuity is the pattern, not the exception.

**The 18-month siege.** Through 2025 the RSF encircled El Fasher, the last SAF-held city in Darfur: Yale Humanitarian Research Lab documented 31 kilometers of RSF-built berms — "a literal kill box" [A: Yale HRL via Guardian]. On October 26, 2025, the city fell. In the first seventy-two hours, the UN later assessed **at least 6,000 civilians killed** (OHCHR, February 2026) — 4,400 inside the city and 1,600 on the exit routes [S: OHCHR, Feb 13, 2026]. El Fasher University and the El-Saudi Hospital became mass killing sites; approximately 500 were killed at the Saudi Maternity Hospital on October 30; fighters filmed themselves executing captives, including **Brig. Gen. Al-Fateh Abdullah Idris, "Abu Lulu"** — the "Butcher of El Fasher," whose TikTok had 144,300 followers [A: MEE, Bellingcat, Sudan Tribune].

**Family separation as a weapon.** Survivors report RSF fighters separating families and killing children in front of parents; boys and young men taken at screening points. Zahra, a mother of six: "They took all the boys" — her 16- and 20-year-old sons were taken at the **Garni** checkpoint; "I don't know if Mohammed is alive or dead" [A: France 24, Nov 1, 2025]. A witness, Rashida Ishaq (New Arab, Nov 4, 2025): fighters executed twenty-plus men including **children under five in a public square near the UNICEF office**, demanding ransoms by phone; her family paid ~$1,700 in gold to escape [B: New Arab]. The Norwegian Refugee Council's Mathilde Vu reported Tawila arrivals "with children who are not their own" — informal absorption mid-flight [A: WaPo/Yahoo].

**Famine declared** in El Fasher on November 3, 2025, with ~260,000 civilians — an estimated 130,000 children — trapped through the siege [A/B: TIME, IPC].

### Phase IV — November 2025–September 2026: The Aftermath Engine

**The detention archipelago.** The fall of El Fasher created a documented system of camps and prisons: the **Garni checkpoint** (25 km northwest of the city) where fleeing civilians were photographed, phones seized, Zaghawa detained on sight — with **Gen. Ali Rizkallah "Savannah"** and **Al-Tijani Ibrahim Musa "Al-Zir Salem"** present, and **Abdelrahim Hamdan Dagalo** personally leading the pursuit of civilians through the trenchworks (UN FFM verified him at the captured 6th Infantry base on October 26, 28, and November 4) [S: UN FFM; B: Sudan War Monitor]. The **El Fasher Children's Hospital** was converted into a detention center holding 2,000+ men, with ~300 deaths, sewage-water drinking, and a cholera-like outbreak killing up to 40 per day; **Shala Prison** held 3,000 men and 500 women by mid-January 2026; the **Al-Mina Al-Bary bus depot** held hundreds of women in ~70 shipping containers, with rape, $2,000 ransoms, at least four girls impregnated by commanders, and forced domestic labor in commanders' homes; **Tagris/Daqris Prison in Nyala** received **6,000+ detainees transferred from El Fasher into a total communications blackout** [A: Al Jazeera, DW; S: Amnesty; B: Sudan Doctors Network]. The Sudan Doctors Network put the citywide toll at **1,500+ killed** since the SAF's flight; WHO reported the kidnapping of four doctors, a pharmacist, and a nurse, held for a **$150,000 ransom**; and MSF's analysts posed the question that still has no official answer: where are the missing who survived the famine and the siege — the numbers do not reconcile. In April 2026 the Sudan Doctors Network documented 370 women and **426 children** among detainees across these sites, with a cholera outbreak since February 2026 [B: Sudan Doctors Network via Al Jazeera].

**Body disposal and the missing.** Bodies were trucked at night toward Zamzam to be burned or buried; witnesses allege the wounded were buried alive and prisoners crushed under vehicles. "Elhadi," 19 (OCHA, April 2026), was detained in the former children's hospital and forced to cart corpses from the Saudi Hospital to mass graves under death threat [S: OCHA]. The Attorney General of Sudan, **Intisar Ahmed Abdel Aal** (February 2026), confirmed mass graves around Khartoum fed by makeshift detention centers — "in schools, universities and public places" — extending to Wad Madani and central Sudan, with exhumations coordinated with the ICRC but stalled by sheer numbers [A: AFP]. Sudan has **no working DNA laboratories**; forensic teams preserve samples by burying them separately in the ground [A: AFP, April 2026].

**The numbers that don't reconcile.** IOM and the Sudan Doctors' Union estimated 177,000 people remained in El Fasher on October 30; the UN counted 82,000 who fled by November 4; only ~10,000 reached Tawila by November 6 — roughly **80,000 people unaccounted for within four days** (analyst Eric Reeves' reconciliation, November 6, 2025) [B: Reeves]. IOM's April 2026 displacement tracking counted **66,169 IDPs still within Al Fasher locality** — which MSF's January 15, 2026 visit (four hours, constant RSF supervision) found to be a "ghost town" of emptied communities [S: MSF; B: Reeves]. The reconciliation has never been published by any official body — **anomaly A3**.

**The Tawila sink.** The displaced converged on Tawila: 700,000 IDPs by end-November 2025, 715,000 by June 2026; the Daba Nayra camp alone held 210,000. Save the Children estimated 450 unaccompanied/separated children (UASC) in Tawila; MedGlobal's estimate was 800. The Washington Post (November 15, 2025) documented babies "clinging to the bodies of their parents," brought to camps by strangers; a 13-year-old girl carried a five-month-old baby with no knowledge of her family's fate [A: WaPo, TIME]. MSF treated 3,396 sexual-violence survivors between January 2024 and November 2025 — 732 of them in December 2025–January 2026 alone in Tawila — with 94% of post-takeover survivors attacked by armed men and **one in five survivors in South Darfur under eighteen, including 41 children under five** [S: MSF, March 31, 2026].

**Abductions in the aftermath.** Reuters' investigation (January 30, 2026): twenty-six witnesses, twenty-three separate incidents, **at least fifty-six children abducted**, ages two months to seventeen, dating to 2023, around El Fasher and across Darfur; in four accounts fighters told families the children "would be used as slaves, to herd animals"; parents were sometimes killed first. Legal experts including Patricia Sellers (former ICC special adviser for slavery crimes) assessed the accounts as potentially constituting "enslavement and slave trading" under international law [A: Reuters]. The New Humanitarian (November 21, 2025) documented the Garni selection system — girls chosen in groups of ~20 and taken to tents at the camp edge — and ransoms of 5 million SDG (~$8,000) per family; OHCHR experts formally flagged trafficking in El Fasher on November 27, 2025 [A/B]. Le Monde (November 7, 2025): "They also captured children… Girls of 11 and 12… To this day, we do not know where they are. We have not found them, not even their bodies" — survivor Mariam [A: Le Monde].

**The UN FFM genocide finding.** On **February 19, 2026**, the UN Fact-Finding Mission on Sudan — chair **Mohamed Chande Othman**, members **Mona Rishmawi** and **Joy Ngozi Ezeilo** — reported that the atrocities in El Fasher "bear the hallmarks of genocide," identifying at least three underlying acts of genocide against the Zaghawa and Fur: killing, causing serious bodily and mental harm, and deliberately inflicting conditions of life calculated to bring about destruction [S: UN FFM]. On September 3, 2026, the FFM's final report named "transnational external support networks" — personnel, training, weapons, logistics — feeding the Zamzam and El Fasher operations; the same report documented the Kalogi drone strike of December 4–5, 2025 (**114 killed, including 46–63 children depending on source — anomaly A4**), the SAF's Ad-Da'ein Teaching Hospital strike (70 killed), and the July–August 2026 drone campaign on El Obeid [S: UN FFM via AP].

### Phase V — 2026: El Obeid, the Live Theater

As this document is compiled, **El Obeid** in North Kordofan is the active crisis: the RSF encircled the city through 2026; UNICEF counted **330 children killed or injured in the first half of 2026 nationwide** (with drones causing 60–80% of child casualties) and 35+ child casualties in North Kordofan since May, including a two-month-old infant; the Security Council issued a statement on June 20, 2026 — "500,000 in danger, imminent risk of mass atrocities"; ~105,000 IDPs were in the city by July, and **39,000 children fled INTO El Obeid in the first half of 2026** (USCRI) — the city as sink, not source, of the next wave [S: UNICEF, UNSC; B: USCRI]. The SAF's recapture of Hamrat al-Wiz (September 6, 2026) partially relieved the encirclement; the UN warned on September 14, 2026 that the humanitarian system "could collapse," with weekend drone strikes displacing thousands across Blue Nile and North/West Darfur [S: UN]. The FFM has opened an urgent inquiry under a July 2026 Human Rights Council resolution [S].

### II.1 The Pattern: A 200-Year Method, Industrialized

The abduction of children in this war is not improvised. It is the reactivation of a documented system:

- **The razzia complex (1821–present).** Slave acquisition "by raid" associated with the Turkiyya; the Baggara confederation (Rizeigat, Misseriya) as the raiding community; captives used for herding, agriculture, domestic labor, and reproductive exploitation. Scholar Jok Madut Jok documented government-armed Baggara militias raiding Dinka territories from 1986 as "low-cost counterinsurgency," with captives forced into labor and what he termed the "forced reproduction of children for the Baggara." ### II.2 The Child-Soldier Economy, Quantified

*Why children specifically? Because they are the cheapest soldier on the market and the hardest to refuse. A grown fighter must be persuaded or paid well; a hungry fourteen-year-old can be bought for less than the price of the rifle he carries, and tribal obligation — faza'a — can be invoked to make the purchase look like duty. The prices below are not metaphors. They are quotes.*

- **Scale.** RSF child recruits estimated at **8,000–10,000** (Sudanese military-intelligence figures cited by the EU Asylum Agency); Sudan's National Committee for War Crimes estimated ~6,000. The UN Secretary-General verified 16 cases in 2024; the 2025 UN Children and Armed Conflict (CAAC) report annexes list the RSF, SAF, and six other armed groups [S: EUAA; S: UN SG report].
- **Prices.** RSF "mayors" (local administrators) are paid **200,000–300,000 SDG ($317–475) per recruited child** per EUAA; **250,000–400,000 SDG ($232–357)** per ACJPS' Nyala fieldwork. Child spies earn ~$10 per reconnaissance mission. SAF "volunteers" aged 15–16 are paid ~$1,000 plus a rifle [S: EUAA; B: ACJPS].
- **Recruitment methods.** Faza'a — "an ancient pre-Islamic Sudanese tradition that allows tribes to rally members… to take revenge" (EUAA) — mobilized through tribal sheikhs; documented coercion includes withholding food and medicine ("enlist or die," verified by the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data project), threats, abduction, torture, and execution of refusals. SAF veterans are coerced; ex-Janajaweed are pressured [S: EUAA; B: ADF].
- **Named recruiters.** Major **Sadam Mohamed Yagoup Al-Nazir** (RSF Military Intelligence; recruited a 15-year-old in June 2023 as an intelligence monitor); **Mohamed Zain** (~200 recruited at once, October 28, 2023, majority children); Sheikh **"Ajeeb"** (tribal leader, Kabra, Central Darfur); **Abdallah Idris "Bata"** (community leader and mayor, Kas locality). - **Training sites.** El Geneina airport camp (May 2025): 300+ children, most under 16 (OHCHR-cited). Nyala: RSF has converted **schools and educational facilities** into assembly and training centers — combat training, drone operations, air-defense exercises; near-daily supply flights from Chad, Libya, and Somaliland since September 2024. Khartoum (July 2024): Colombian contractors training children in and around the capital; defectors and a SAF officer describe children **chained to anti-aircraft guns, beaten, and forced to fire artillery at drone-designated targets including civilian neighborhoods** [S: OHCHR; A: AP/HRW; B: Nyala documentation].

## PART III — THE MONEY MAP: WHERE EVERY DOLLAR COMES FROM AND WHERE IT GOES

*Part II named what a child costs. This part answers the question that follows: who can afford to pay? The answer is a war that pays for itself — a loop that turns the country's own earth into weapons and the weapons into control of more earth. Follow the loop and you will understand why this war has no financial reason to end.*

### III.1 Gold: The Engine

*Think of the gold loop as a laundromat that pays for its own electricity. Dirty metal leaves the mine; it passes through a refinery's door and comes out legally clean; the clean metal is sold, and the proceeds return as weapons, fuel, and salaries — which capture more mines. The washing is not hidden in the dark; it happens in licensed businesses, under trade rules, in broad daylight. That is what makes it so hard to stop: every participant can show you a receipt.*

- **Production and leakage.** Finance Minister **Jibril Ibrahim** (2026): production crashed to 6.4t in 2023, rebounded to a record in 2025 — the World Gold Council figure is **74.6 tonnes yielded** — but **only 12.5t entered the public treasury; over 80% of production was smuggled; 55 tonnes "vanished into the shadow economy."** Artisanal mining is 80–90% of output, entirely outside state control [S: ministry statements via Sudan Tribune/Eastleigh Voice; A: WGC].
- **The UAE share.** Official Sudanese gold exports to the UAE: **$1.97 billion in 2024**; 96–97% of Sudan's gold reaches the UAE. Swissaid estimates undeclared artisanal flows to Dubai at up to **$30 billion/year**; its researchers recorded a 70% increase in informal UAE-bound flows in 2024 [S/A: CESS, Swissaid].
- **The heist.** On May 24, 2023, the RSF looted the Central Bank of Sudan and the state refinery: the gold minister's accounting lists **1,273 kg seized (including 906 kg of company balances)** plus 15 tonnes of silver; a Financial Times investigation (August 2026, Katharine Houreld) established that **$100M+ in bullion — 1.5+ tonnes — was flown out and smuggled to the UAE** [S: ministry; S/A: FT].
- **The laundering loophole.** Gold is classified under the London Bullion Market Association's "last country of processing" rule: Dubai gold re-refined in Switzerland becomes "Swiss." The flow: **316 tonnes, CHF 27 billion, January–September 2025** from the UAE to four Swiss refiners — **Valcambi, PAMP, Argor-Heraeus, Metalor**. Valcambi — the world's largest refinery — sourced ~20t directly from **Kaloti** (the Dubai refiner the US DEA recommended for Patriot Act money-laundering designation in 2014, which the US Treasury shelved to avoid angering the UAE — former officials' testimony to the ICIJ) plus 60t via **Trust One Financial Services** (Kaloti family directors) after Swiss regulators warned in writing of "very significant risks." New LBMA country-of-origin disclosure rules were announced January 2026, mandatory by 2027. A former EY partner's whistleblowing on the Kaloti-Valcambi relationship was upheld in UK court [A: ICIJ, Reuters, Swissaid; B: EY case record]. Volkswagen appears in the chain via a Kaloti-linked smelter reference (CID002567) [B: chain-of-custody documentation].
- **Sanctions wave.** EU Regulation 2026/1724 (July 13, 2026): ban on Sudanese gold imports (direct and via third countries) plus mercury and cyanide export bans — a Dutch-French initiative with a dynamic list mechanism. UK (July 16, 2026): four persons and seven entities, including state miners **Omdurman Mining, Sudamin, and Ariab** (channeling gold profits to both warring factions) and **Portex Trade Limited (Hong Kong)**. Switzerland aligned September 10, 2026. On **May 10, 2026**, Sudanese victims — represented by the **DEMAIN** law firm — filed a formal EU sanctions request naming Emirati officials including **Sheikh Mansour**, alongside Fadlalla, Alhammadi, Al Marri, Habib, and Hashim, and the entities GSSG, Al-Fakher, Prodigious, and Aoun. The Sentry's asks (July 24, 2026): network designations, enhanced due-diligence on UAE gold, and a centralized gold data initiative [S: EU, UK, Switzerland; A: Sentry].
- **The corridors.** RSF-held mines (Jebel Amer — retaken at the war's start; **Songo/Sungu**, the most productive, defended by RSF South Darfur commander **Saleh al-Futi** with **Wagner/Africa Corps** mercenaries manning anti-aircraft positions at the Al Junaid site) feed the Nyala gold market, then: Raja airport (South Sudan); the **Kenya corridor** — where the Fikra for Studies report (May 2026) quantified the Kenya→UAE gold-export gap at **+1,719% post-war**, "statistically inexplicable" — prompting President Ruto's raw-gold export ban and three-refinery plan (September 16, 2026); Chad; CAR. Chatham House identifies three smuggling modes: direct air courier, mislabeling, and overland via neighbors [A/B: Fikra, Chatham House; B: Noria].
- **The Saudi pivot.** January 2026: the Saudi Gold Refining Company told Sudan it is ready to buy gold immediately — the first direct challenge to Dubai's monopoly and the economic expression of the Saudi–UAE rupture [B: Middle East coverage].
- **War economics.** Geopolitical Monitor (March 2026): RSF revenue estimated at **$1–2B/year**; RSF-area trade generates **$2.5–3M/day**; Sudan's parallel (black) market hit **$4B and doubled in 2025**. ORSAM's framing: Sudan's war economy is a **"transit economy"** — revenue from transit routes matters more than production, and relabeling through neighbors defeats traceability [B: Geopolitical Monitor; A: ORSAM].

### III.2 The Other Commodity Streams

- **Gum arabic.** OHCHR briefing paper (July 15, 2026, S): 3,700 tonnes looted January–June 2024 as fighter compensation; the RSF "formalized" extortion into fees plus armed escorts; the El-Nuhud Gum Arabic Exchange was looted in May 2025; losses in Al-Nahud alone estimated at $125M; truck levies of $2,000; routes run RSF areas → Souq al-Na'am → Juba → Mombasa, and Chad → Douala; looted gum is relabeled in transit states as national product. - **Fuel.** The UAE supplies **84% of Sudan's fuel imports** (CESS, April 2026) — the symmetry is exact: gold flows one way, fuel the other [S: CESS].
- **Captagon.** After the fall of the Assad regime (December 2024) closed the Syrian pipeline, Sudan's production capacity surged fourteen-fold in twenty months inside RSF territory (Geopolitical Monitor, flagged for independent confirmation) [B/C].
- **Loot.** The RSF's "Dagalo markets" around Nyala (Shefshefa, Kajik, Garda) sold looted goods — televisions at $10–20, laptops at $5–7, vehicles into Chad; April–July 2023 looting was estimated at tens of billions of dollars; WFP lost $60M in food stocks. Sudan's National Museum was 60–90% destroyed or looted — **Napatan and Meroitic gold jewellery removed** — feeding the same antiquities pipelines as the bullion; the El Geneina museum's curator, **Bakri Hassan Ali**, was killed [B: ISS/ENACT; A: museum documentation].
- **Land.** The Gulf food-security portfolio in Sudan: UAE ~$10B, Saudi ~$1B, Qatar ~$500M in agricultural and livestock assets; **Qatar Mining** holds copper concessions (~$800M). The International Holding Company (IHC) and Jenaan farmed **50,000+ hectares during the war itself**; the **IHC–DAL Group** deal covers **162,000 hectares at Abu Hamad** ($225M) with a 500-km corridor road to a new Red Sea port to be built and operated by AD Ports; the UAE has pumped **$6B+** into Sudanese reserves, agriculture, and port infrastructure. The RSX1 shipping service links Port Sudan to Jebel Ali. GRAIN (March 2025): "The UAE's top interest is Sudan's gold, but food is also a key priority." Sudanese farmer organizer **Abdelraouf Omer** and GRAIN document 70% of Gezira smallholders displaced [A/B: GRAIN; B: Coface].
- **Mercenary payroll.** $2,600/month contracts for Colombians; bonuses paid when El Fasher fell (FT, August 2026); a UAE court document in the SAF-side case records a $13M deal with $3M in kickbacks [A: FT; B: court reporting].

The dual-currency reality is a deliberate act of state fragmentation — and a documented one [B: Ayin, Sudan Tribune; S: central-bank warnings].

*A currency is a promise that a state will exist tomorrow. By printing its own money — and backdating the signatures — Tasis is not counterfeiting; it is counterfeiting the future. It is teaching everyone inside its territory that the real Sudan is already gone. And the same logic runs through the exam halls: whoever controls the currency controls what people can buy; whoever controls the certificates controls what the next generation knows to be true. A child who passes a Tasis exam has been given an identity that the rest of the world does not recognize — which is precisely the condition of the disappeared.*

### IV.2 Evasion Mechanics (the documented playbook)

1. **Ownership cycling** — shareholdings shifted to proxies days before or after designations, leaving digital footprints identical to the originals [A: The Sentry].
2. **Last-country-of-processing laundering** — conflict gold becomes "Swiss" (or "Emirati") at the refinery gate [A: ICIJ/Swissaid].
3. **Airline layering** — aircraft re-registered across Burkina Faso, the CAR, and Kyrgyzstan; operating companies (Gewan/Invicta/Batot) interposed between the true owner and the flight [A: Le Monde].

5. **End-user certificate abuse** — the Dunarit/IGG pattern: arms bought on UAE Ministry of Defense certificates, diverted in theater; Bulgaria's knowledge documented since 2016 [A: France 24].
6. **Counter-projection** — the UAE's own State Security Court prosecutes *the other side's* smuggling (the April 30, 2026 case: SAF chief of staff **Gen. Yassir al-Atta charged in absentia**, **Salah Gosh** named, coordinator **Osman Mohamed al-Zubair**, thirteen defendants and six companies — **Rashed Omar Brokerage, Portex Trade Limited (Hong Kong), Wardat Al Masarra Trading, Sudamina, Yellow Sand Trading, Apollara Electronics Trading** — $13M deal with $3M in kickbacks, audio recordings, confessions, bank transfers) [B: court reporting, Africa Confidential].

## PART VI — THE ATROCITY ARSENAL

*Methods evolve. This part catalogs the weapons layer of the disappearance system — the drones that now kill most of the civilians, the chlorine bombs documented in a general's own text messages, the mass graves, and something less visible than any of them: the destruction of identity itself. A child whose paperwork is erased is a child the world cannot find even when the world starts looking.*

### VI.1 The Drone War

- **Scale.** More than 1,000 drone attacks since April 2023 (ACLED); 198 strikes in January–February 2026 alone, 52 with civilian casualties, 478 killed; Sudan accounts for over half of Africa's drone attacks. Drones caused roughly **60–80% of civilian war deaths in 2026**; of ~1,400 recorded civilian deaths in the first half of 2026, more than 1,100 were drone-related. No supplier state — UAE, Iran, Turkey, Russia, or China — has ever been sanctioned for Darfur embargo violations in this war [A/B: ACLED; B: analysis].
- **Named strikes.** Kalogi (December 4–5, 2025): 114 killed including children — figures range from 43 to 63 children depending on source (**anomaly A4**). The Shukeiri school strike (RSF). The Zamzam and El Fasher sieges involved drone-directed artillery.
- **First-of-its-kind air combat.** May 2026: an Akinci drone operated by Sudan downed another Akinci operated by the UAE's side — Turkish hardware flown against itself [B: aerospace trade press].
- **The electronic-warfare (EW) layer.** RSF Chinese CH-95 drones (assessed UAE-supplied) and jammer and surface-to-air missile (SAM) systems (PAX's Wim Zwijnenburg) downed at least four Akincis (~$25M each) [B: PAX; A: NYT].

### VI.2 Chemical Weapons

- **The dossier.** Washington Post/New York Times (September 5, 2026), sourced to a Middle Eastern intelligence agency (MEE and Le Monde identify it as Israeli): ~150 documents, thousands of intercepted messages from senior officers' phones. The program: led by **Brig. Gen. Tariq Hussain**; weaponization of chlorine proposed July 2024; munition ~15kg chlorine plus ~20kg explosives; first test flown from **Merowe Air Base** July 2024 — Hussain's message: "The test went 100 percent"; ~290 munitions produced by October 2024; **106 used in combat**; chlorine diverted from an Omdurman water-treatment plant that had received it from international aid groups; usage at least six months, July 2024–January 2025; digital-forensics veterans found no manipulation; a video of an apparent victim with charred skin was texted to an airstrike officer: "Your cooking" [A: WaPo/NYT; B: MEE, Le Monde].
- **The cover-up.** After US May 2025 sanctions, intercepted messages indicate **Burhan ordered Hussain to "remove all traces."** The military's investigative committee — which **included Hussain as an adviser** — reported finding nothing [A: WaPo/NYT].
- **Consequences and counter-allegations.** The US sanctioned Burhan in January 2025 on a chemical-weapons determination; further US CBW Act sanctions followed June 23, 2026; a US draft resolution would expand the embargo; the RSF in turn alleges SAF chemical use (Shangili Tobai, June 2025). France 24 had earlier documented apparent chemical use at the **El Jeili refinery** (September 2024). Sudan's September 2025 complaint to the UN referenced an eighteen-page Spanish-language operational order for the RSF's "Desert Wolves" battalion dated December 1, 2024, which included white-phosphorus allegations and photographs of child training — an allegation, filed formally, not independently verified. Both sides' allegations require independent verification [S: US Treasury/State; A; flagged].

### VI.3 Mass Graves and Exhumations

- **The record.** Attorney General **Intisar Ahmed Abdel Aal** (February 2026): mass graves around Khartoum fed by makeshift detention centers; bodies "in schools, universities and public places"; graves extend to Wad Madani and central Sudan; exhumations coordinated with the ICRC, stalled by numbers [A: AFP].
- **El Fasher.** Bodies trucked at night toward Zamzam; the former children's hospital site shows evidence consistent with mass killing; Elhadi's forced corpse-clearing account (Part I); Yale HRL's 31+ cluster sites [A/B].
- **The forensic vacuum.** No working DNA laboratories; samples buried in the ground; an ICRC-facilitated study visit of Sudanese medico-legal professionals to Iraq (the world's reference case for mass-grave processing) is the only capacity bridge documented [A: AFP; S: ICRC].

### VI.4 Identity Destruction

- **Birth registration.** Requires the father's identity and national number; babies born of rape are systematically undocumented; the El Fasher civil registry destroyed; Tasis exam committees "verify" documentless students — a parallel credential system [A: BBC; B].
- **The Mygoma paper trail.** The approximately 275 children dispersed into foster care in Gezira, Sennar, and Kassala states entered that system largely without documents; the destination of the December 2023 evacuation (253 children) is unnamed in any public source [S/A; **anomalies A1–A2; gap G4**].
- **Telecommunications.** The RSF seized ISP data centers (February 2024); outages persist across both zones; the RSF rents Starlink to civilians while using it for drone operations; the regulator terminated the Thuraya license (February 1, 2026). Internet and telephony are the infrastructure of testimony — its destruction is part of the atrocity method [B: Sudan telecom reporting].

*Step back and see these four subsections as one machine. The drones make witnesses flee; the mass graves remove the evidence of the dead; the destroyed registries remove the evidence of the living; the severed networks remove the witnesses' voices. A person without documents is a person without a country — unable to prove a crime happened to them, unable to claim an inheritance, unable to cross a border legally, unable to be traced. Paper is not bureaucracy in this story. Paper is the person.*

## PART VII — THE OUTFLOW: TRAFFICKING CORRIDORS AND HOST-COUNTRY SYSTEMS

*Not every taken child stays inside the war. This part follows the roads north — the Nile crossing, the Sahara pistes, the detention warehouses of Libya — and the systems that receive children at the other end: the refugee bureaucracy of Egypt, the foster-and-kafala market, the strip of Mediterranean beach where the boats leave. The receiving systems are where the world's leverage is greatest, because they operate in countries that answer to courts and parliaments.*

### VII.1 The Route Map (documented)

- **Egypt.** The primary destination: **1.5 million Sudanese** (official figure, January 29, 2026), of whom **834,201 are UNHCR-registered** (December 2025); approximately 42% of the registered are children; **7,485 unaccompanied/separated children (UASC) identified by June 2024** of 18,196 children at risk — only 41% received Best Interests Procedures. Asylum-residence wait: **576 days** (mid-2024); two of three registration centers closed; appointments pushed to 2028–29; UNHCR Egypt cash assistance 98% unfunded (April 14, 2026) [S: UNHCR, OHCHR].
- **The Egyptian state machinery.** A new asylum law (December 16, 2024); an October 2025 surge in arrests and deportations; **leaked government deportation documents** instructing confiscation of UNHCR cards and coerced "voluntary departure" declarations; deportations without individualized refoulement assessment; a 17-year-old deported in January 2026 (arrested walking home from school); sweeps at metro stations, workplaces, and refugee-run service centers; dozens of Sudanese-run schools shut. **EIPR (February 25, 2026): seven refugee/migrant deaths in detention since January 2026, at least five UNHCR-registered** — including a Sudanese child arrested buying food who died awaiting deportation processing, and 67-year-old **Mubarak Qamar al-Din**, who died at Shorouk Police Station despite a valid UNHCR card [S: OHCHR (March 6, 2026); A: New Humanitarian; B: EIPR].
- **The UNHCR compromise.** A current staffer's allegations of Egyptian security infiltration of UNHCR operations — cases buried, complaints blocked; a registration-slot black market operating "daily and in plain sight of the police"; agency data on forced returns assessed as "very weak" and contradicted by everyone else's documentation [A: New Humanitarian, December 4, 2025 — flagged: insider allegation].
- **The return trains.** Since July 21, 2025, Egyptian National Railways and Sudan's DIS co-run "voluntary return" trains; **190,000+ Sudanese crossed back January–July 2025 — five times all of 2024**. The Emergency Lawyers (Noon Kashkoush) call the returns unsafe: RSF drone strikes near the border, cholera, armed robbery, collapsed services. Children without documents have been stranded at Wadi Halfa [B: Mada Masr, July 2026; Emergency Lawyers].
- **Exploitation typologies in Egypt (US State Department's annual Trafficking in Persons (TIP) report, S).** "Transactional/summer marriages" of girls to Gulf men (including Emiratis) facilitated by parents and brokers; **Sudanese gangs coerce UASC into drug sales and petty crime**; women and girls fleeing Sudan coerced into sex acts for visas; smugglers exploit UASC in sex trafficking, domestic servitude, and construction; Sudanese domestic workers excluded from labor-law protection. The Guardian (September 7, 2026) documents the online layer: fake job offers via Facebook Messenger ("employment in Egypt") that deliver women and girls into sexual exploitation [S: US TIP 2025; A: Guardian].
- **The kafala market structure.** Academic documentation of Egypt's care system: "families come looking for a child as if they are buying a product… a ready-made child"; a placement hierarchy that selects healthy infants and excludes older or disabled children; secondary abandonment; youth describing institutional transfers as **"Subbouba" (profiteering)** — children moved to unlock grant cycles; Egypt recorded ~12,300 adopted children and ~12,000 foster families as of January 2025; a standalone Alternative Care Law remains unenacted; kafala preserves lineage (Nasab), which under Islamic law prohibits full adoption and creates a permanent second-class status [B: AUC thesis; Egypt statistics; Better Care Network].
- **Libya.** UNHCR (March 2026): **559,920 Sudanese refugees in Libya since 2023**; 6,101 people detained countrywide; UNHCR's alternative-care network — the formal net for the entire Sudanese UASC population of Libya — consists of **43 caregivers hosting 109 refugees, of whom 89 are UASC** [S: UNHCR]. OHCHR/UNSMIL (February 17, 2026): "a violent business model… business as usual," with trafficking networks holding "ties to Libyan authorities"; girls as young as fourteen raped daily in Tobruk trafficking houses [S: OHCHR/UNSMIL]. IOM (January 2026): an Ajdabiya mass grave (21 bodies; 195 freed) and a **Kufra underground site three meters deep** (221 freed, including a one-month-old baby) [S: IOM]. Dabanga (July 2025): hundreds of Sudanese children aged 5–16 on Tripoli's streets; Sudanese community leaders warn of organ-trafficking networks; **UNICEF pays Sudanese families monthly stipends to absorb children** — a cash-incentivized, unregulated absorption market; school fees of $170–180 drive dropout; forced begging under threat of eviction; boys held with adult men in detention [B: Dabanga; B: GI-TOC]. Sudan Tribune (September 17–18, 2026): ~1,900 Sudanese in Libyan detention centers amid abuse allegations [B]. Organ-harvesting allegations recur in survivor testimony (Reuters, March 2025) — **unverified; flagged** [A: Reuters].

**Eastern Sudan (SAF-held).** The Kassala–Gedaref–Red Sea axis hosts 500,000+ displaced in Kassala alone (the Minaa El Bari site among them); the US State Department's trafficking reports have long documented syndicates operating along the Ethiopian and Eritrean borders, cross-border kidnapping incidents, and rising child labor. Sudan's National Council for Child Welfare (June 2026) assessed **71% of Sudanese children to be in severe danger** [S: US TIP; B: NCCR].

**The pre-war baseline — this economy predates the war.** The US State Department's pre-war trafficking reports documented: homeless Khartoum children exploited in sex trafficking and forced begging; child labor in brick factories, gold mining, waste collection, and street vending; officials of the Commission of Refugees and the General Intelligence Service sexually exploiting refugees; and corrupt border guards inside trafficking networks. Historical routes: Rashaida smugglers abducting people from Khartoum's refugee camps; Darfuri girls recruited by force into SAF households; children trafficked via Yemen to Saudi Arabia for begging; Sudanese women trafficked as far as Poland.
